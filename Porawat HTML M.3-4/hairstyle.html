HAIR / FACE / STYLE

YOUR HAIR FRAMES YOUR FACE.

A good hairstyle isn't simply the haircut that is trending. It should work with your face shape, hair texture, density, lifestyle and the amount of maintenance you're willing to do.

THE FOUNDATION

START
WITH YOUR HAIR.

01

TEXTURE

Straight, wavy, curly and coily hair behave differently. Choose styles that work with your natural texture instead of constantly fighting it.

02

FACE
SHAPE

Hair changes the visual balance of your face. Length, volume and shape can be used to create a more balanced overall appearance.

03

MAINTENANCE

The best haircut is one you can consistently maintain. A complicated style that never gets styled is rarely better than a simpler haircut that always looks good.

01 — FACE SHAPE

FIND YOUR
BALANCE.

Face shape should be treated as a starting point rather than a strict rulebook. Hair volume, facial hair, styling and individual features can all change the final appearance.

01

OVAL

An oval face is relatively balanced, which gives you considerable flexibility. Many short, medium and longer styles can work.

02

ROUND

Consider styles that add some height or structure on top while keeping excessive width at the sides under control.

03

SQUARE

Strong jawlines often work well with structured cuts, but softer textured styles can also create a more relaxed appearance.

04

OBLONG

Avoid automatically adding excessive height if you want to visually balance a longer face. Moderate volume and some width can work well.

05

HEART

Styles that create balanced volume around the sides and lower portions of the face can work particularly well.

06

DIAMOND

Textured styles with controlled volume can help balance prominent cheekbones and a narrower forehead or jaw.

HAIR TEXTURE

WORK
WITH IT.

01

STRAIGHT

Straight hair often shows shape and structure clearly. Layering, texture and controlled volume can prevent it from looking excessively flat.

02

WAVY

Wavy hair naturally provides movement. Avoid overloading it with heavy products and consider styles that allow the natural wave to remain visible.

03

CURLY

Curly hair benefits from moisture, shape and appropriate layering. A good stylist should understand how curls behave before cutting them.

02 — CHOOSING A CUT

DON'T JUST
SHOW A
PHOTO.

Bringing a reference photo is useful, but your hair may not behave like the model's hair. A better barber conversation includes your texture, density, face shape, preferred length and how much time you want to spend styling.

01

SHOW MULTIPLE REFERENCES

Find several examples that share the shape or characteristics you want instead of relying on a single photograph.

02

EXPLAIN YOUR ROUTINE

Tell your barber whether you want something that takes five minutes or twenty minutes to style every morning.

03

BE HONEST ABOUT LENGTH

If you don't want to spend months growing your hair out, say so. Your barber can suggest a version that works with your current length.

04

ASK WHAT SUITS YOU

A good barber can identify practical adjustments based on your hair's density, growth pattern, texture and your desired appearance.

03

GREAT HAIR
STARTS WITH
HEALTHY HABITS.

Styling can improve appearance, but it cannot compensate for consistently poor hair care. Build the foundation first.

03 — HAIRCARE

BUILD YOUR
ROUTINE.

01

WASH APPROPRIATELY

Washing frequency depends on your scalp, hair type and lifestyle. The goal is a clean, comfortable scalp without unnecessarily irritating or drying your hair.

02

CONDITION

Conditioner can help improve manageability and reduce dryness. Apply it according to your hair type and product instructions.

03

DON'T ABUSE HEAT

Frequent high-heat styling can damage hair. Use appropriate settings and avoid repeatedly overheating the same sections.

04

MAINTAIN YOUR CUT

A haircut can lose its intended shape as it grows. Schedule trims based on the style rather than automatically following a fixed calendar.

05

TAKE CARE OF YOUR SCALP

Persistent itching, severe dandruff, sudden shedding or other unusual scalp changes are reasons to seek professional advice rather than simply adding more styling products.

STYLING

MAKE IT
WORK.

01

MATTE

Matte products generally create a less shiny, more natural finish. They can work particularly well for textured or structured styles.

02

VOLUME

Blow-drying and appropriate styling products can help create volume. Start with a small amount of product and build gradually.

03

TEXTURE

Texture creates separation and movement. Avoid using so much product that individual strands become heavy or greasy-looking.

04 — COMMON MISTAKES

STOP
OVERDOING IT.

01

TOO MUCH PRODUCT

More product does not automatically mean better styling. Start small and add only when necessary.

02

IGNORING YOUR TEXTURE

Trying to force naturally curly or wavy hair into a completely different texture every day can require excessive heat and manipulation.

03

COPYING SOMEONE ELSE

A hairstyle can look excellent on someone else and still be wrong for your hair or proportions. Use inspiration rather than copying blindly.

04

NO MAINTENANCE

Even a great haircut can look messy if the surrounding routine is neglected. Keep your hair clean, shaped and styled consistently.
